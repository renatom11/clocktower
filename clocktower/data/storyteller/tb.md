# Storyteller's notes: Trouble Brewing

Compiled for the Storyteller judge from the Trouble Brewing almanac's How to Run sections and tips, the rulebook
and the wiki, in our own words. One section per character: what the Storyteller decides for it, the interactions
to watch on this script, and how a good Storyteller handles them. The judge reads the sections for the characters
in play.

## Washerwoman
You choose the pair: the real Townsfolk and one other player (any alignment). A Spy may be shown as a Townsfolk
here; showing the Spy as the Townsfolk they claim makes their bluff look confirmed. A drunk or poisoned Washerwoman
is best shown a pair that vouches for an evil player as a good character, which misleads the town for days.
Never show the Washerwoman their own character as the pair's Townsfolk.

## Librarian
You choose the pair: a real Outsider and one other player, or "zero" when no Outsiders are in play. The Drunk is
shown as the Drunk, not as the Townsfolk they think they are. A Recluse may register as a Minion and be left out,
and a Spy may register as an Outsider. "Zero" is a strong statement: with a Baron, or a Drunk, in play the town will
count Outsiders, so false information should fit the setup's counts. A Librarian who learns the Drunk confirms that
the Drunk's player is not what they claim, which is usually a big swing for good.

## Investigator
You choose the pair: a real Minion and one other player, usually a good one so the clue is not obviously the pair
of evil players. A Recluse may register as a Minion here, which frames them; a Spy may register as good and be left
out. A poisoned Investigator works best when a good player is framed as a Minion.

## Chef
You give the number of adjacent evil pairs. The Spy and the Recluse may each register either way, and a Recluse
between two evil players can count for one pair and not the other. Avoid a misregistration that makes the count
give the game away in a small circle. A poisoned Chef's number should be plausible for the setup (no more pairs
than the evil team could make).

## Empath
Each night you give how many of the two alive neighbours are evil, skipping dead players. The Recluse and the Spy
may register either way, and you may change your mind from night to night. A drunk or poisoned Empath should get a
number that misleads without contradicting itself night after night, unless the change is the point (the Poisoner
moved). Watch the Empath's neighbours change as players die: the next living player counts.

## Fortune Teller
At setup you choose the red herring, a good player who registers as the Demon to the Fortune Teller all game;
usually a Townsfolk, which frames a useful player; in small games the Fortune Teller may be their own. The Recluse
may register as the Demon. A dead Demon still gives a yes. A drunk Fortune Teller (the Drunk) keeps a red herring
too. Poisoned results should usually be false, and point at a good player rather than clear the Demon in an
obvious way.

## Undertaker
After a day's execution kills someone, you show the executed player's character. A Spy may register as a good
character (supporting a sacrificed Spy's claim), a Recluse as a Minion or Demon. If the executed Demon claimed a
Townsfolk and a Scarlet Woman took over, a poisoned Undertaker who sees that claim supports evil's story. Show the
Imp only if it fits the game going on (a Scarlet Woman could have taken over, five or more alive).

## Monk
The Monk protects one other player from the Demon tonight; you just honour it, and at dawn say nobody died. A
Monk-protected Imp choosing themself does not die. The Demon may learn the Monk's pattern from a Spy. Do not say why
nobody died: a Soldier, the Monk, or a Mayor bounce onto a protected player all look the same.

## Ravenkeeper
If killed at night they choose a player and learn the character. The Spy may register as any good character (show
the one they claim), the Recluse as any evil one. A drunk or poisoned Ravenkeeper is best shown an evil player's
bluff, or an evil character for a good player (the rulebook's example). A Ravenkeeper who chooses themself learns
what they think they are.

## Virgin
The first nomination of the Virgin executes the nominator at once if they are a Townsfolk. The Drunk is an Outsider
and does not trigger it. The Spy may register as a Townsfolk: a Spy nominating the Virgin is usually a deliberate
sacrifice to look like a proven Townsfolk, so if the Spy is claiming a Townsfolk, letting the Virgin execute them
honours both abilities and their bluff; if they claim an Outsider, registering as an Outsider fits. The ability is
spent even if the Virgin is poisoned or the nominator is not a Townsfolk.

## Slayer
Once per game, by day, the Slayer shoots a player: an alive Demon dies. The Recluse may register as the Demon and
die, which makes the town think the Demon died and a Scarlet Woman took over: a heavy blow to good. Decide it by
the state of the game: when good is far ahead it keeps the game alive; when good is behind it is cruel. Anyone may
claim a shot; nothing happens for a fake Slayer, which is how evil bluffs it.

## Soldier
Safe from the Demon; at dawn say nobody died. A poisoned Soldier can die. Nothing to decide, but a Spy can tell the
Demon who the Soldier is, and a Mayor bounce should not land on a sober Soldier (it would fail anyway).

## Mayor
If the Demon attacks the Mayor, you choose whether the Mayor dies or someone else does. Most Storytellers keep the
Mayor alive into the final day, which is most fun; if the town is overwhelmingly convinced early that the Mayor is
real, letting the Mayor die gives evil a chance. A bounce usually lands on a good player (sometimes the Minion, very
rarely), and should look like an ordinary kill. At three alive with no execution the Mayor's team wins at dusk.

## Butler
Each night the Butler chooses a master; tomorrow they may vote only while the master votes. Count a Butler's
illegal vote anyway rather than reveal them. A poisoned or drunk Butler may vote freely, but does not know it.

## Drunk
At setup you choose which Townsfolk (not in play) the Drunk believes they are; information roles are usual, since
false results mislead most, and avoid one of the Demon's bluffs (a sure double claim). Their information can be
true or false: keep their story consistent from night to night, usually false, and in a way that fits evil's
bluffs. A Librarian or Undertaker sees them as the Drunk.

## Recluse
Each time an ability detects or affects evil players, you decide whether the Recluse registers as evil, and as which
Minion or Demon. It can be shown as a Minion to the Investigator, as evil to the Chef and Empath, as the Demon to
the Fortune Teller and the Slayer, as an evil character to the Undertaker and Ravenkeeper. Use it to mislead
without making the game unwinnable for good; consistency helps the Recluse's own claim be believed.

## Saint
If executed while sober, evil wins. Nothing to decide, but a poisoned Saint's execution does not lose, and the
Poisoner may poison the Saint to let the town execute them safely, or the reverse. Watch for evil bluffing as the
Saint to avoid execution.

## Poisoner
Each night the Poisoner poisons a player until dusk tomorrow. The poisoned player's information is yours to choose:
usually false, harmful to them and supporting evil's bluffs. A poison that lands on an information role the night
before an evil bluff is checked should be rewarded. A dead or changed Poisoner's poison ends.

## Spy
The Spy sees the Grimoire each night and may register as good, and as a Townsfolk or Outsider, to every ability.
Show it as the character it claims (the wiki's advice), so its bluff is supported; it can register as a Townsfolk
to the Virgin when it sacrifices itself. Keep the Grimoire readable when shown. A poisoned Spy registers as itself.

## Scarlet Woman
If the Demon dies with five or more players alive (Travellers aside), she becomes the Demon. A Slayer's shot on the
Recluse, or a starpass, can look like this. The Imp choosing themself passes to the Scarlet Woman first. Do not
announce it; she learns it privately.

## Baron
Two Townsfolk become Outsiders at setup (if one is the Drunk, follow the Drunk's setup too). Information that counts
Outsiders (the Librarian's "zero", a poisoned Librarian's pair) must fit the extra Outsiders, and misinformation
should name the Baron only when the Outsider count allows it. Evil bluffs should not break the Outsider count
either: with a Baron in play the town expects more Outsiders, so an Outsider bluff fits.

## Imp
Each night the Imp kills a player. If the Imp kills themself, a Minion becomes the Imp: the Scarlet Woman if she
can, otherwise you choose a living Minion, usually the one best placed to carry on (one with a good bluff, not
under suspicion). The Mayor may bounce the kill; the Monk and Soldier stop it.

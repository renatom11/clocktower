# Storyteller's notes: Sects & Violets

Compiled for the Storyteller judge from the Sects & Violets almanac's How to Run sections and examples, the
rulebook (madness, alignment and character) and the wiki, in our own words. One section per character: what the
Storyteller decides for it, the interactions to watch on this script, and how a good Storyteller handles them. In
Sects & Violets the town must first work out which Demon is in play; characters and alignments change, Minions act
visibly, and information is often false. Never say why something happened. The judge reads the sections for the
characters in play.

## Witch
Each night the Witch curses a player; if that player nominates tomorrow, they die at once, and their nomination still
goes to a vote. The curse lasts one day; the same player can be cursed again. Once only three players are alive the
Witch loses the ability at once, a curse already made included. A drunk or poisoned Witch's curse is wasted; a sober
Witch's curse pauses while the Witch is drunk and comes back when they sober up. A curse follows the player through
a character change (a cursed Klutz who becomes the Fang Gu dies nominating, and good wins). A Witch killed by the
Vigormortis keeps cursing. Nothing for you to decide; the visible deaths tell the town a Witch is about.

## Cerenovus
Each night the Cerenovus picks a player (alive or dead) and a good character; that player is told, and must spend
tomorrow trying to convince the group they are that character. Judge madness like humour: by effort and intent, not
by a word test. A sincere claim, defended, with the character's "information" made up, keeps them safe, even if
nobody believes it; saying "no" when asked whether they are mad is right. Silence, one token line, a hint or wink, a
private confession you overhear, claiming something else, or saying "I am mad" breaks it. A player who breaks it
might be executed: usually yes for a good player (it is their choice, even if it ends the game: a mad good twin
executed loses it for good), more sparingly for an evil player, so evil can't use a madness execution to end the day
before a dangerous nomination. A madness execution is the day's one execution, and it counts against a Vortox. A
drunk or poisoned Cerenovus makes nobody mad. The Cerenovus rarely picks the Demon.

## Pit-Hag
Each night but the first, the Pit-Hag picks a player and a character on the script; if no one has that character
(a dead player's counts), the player becomes it and is told, keeping their alignment (a good Witch, an evil
Townsfolk). The new character's ability is fresh. If a Demon is made, deaths tonight are yours to choose, as the
Pit-Hag's attacks: a Demon turned into another Demon, it is best that nobody dies tonight, so the town sees the
change (you may even cancel the Demon's attack); a new evil Demon, kill the old Demon (the new one still attacks);
a good Demon, kill the evil Demon if it is the final night, otherwise let both run, remembering all Demons must die
for good to win. Turning the Demon into a non-Demon ends the game for good unless an Evil Twin holds it. A new
Evil Twin is paired at once with a player of the other alignment, and both learn each other. A Pit-Hag killed by
the Vigormortis keeps acting until the Vigormortis loses its ability.

## Evil Twin
At setup you pick the twin: any good player. On the first night both learn each other: the twin learns who the Evil
Twin is, the Evil Twin the twin's character. Executing the good one of the two (whichever character they have) wins
for evil while the Evil Twin has their ability; executing the evil one does nothing special; good cannot win while
both live, so a dead Demon with both twins alive means the game goes on, with no more night deaths. A dead Evil Twin
has no ability (unless the Vigormortis killed them). If the twins end up on the same side (a Fang Gu jump into the
twin), pick a new twin of the other alignment and tell both. A new Evil Twin on the final night should be paired with
the Demon or a dead player, so good can still win.

## Fang Gu
Each night but the first, the Fang Gu kills. The first Outsider it would kill instead becomes an evil Fang Gu and the
old Fang Gu dies; this happens once a game, whatever happens later. The new Fang Gu learns their character and that
they are evil, but not who the Minions are. If the Outsider would not die (dead already, protected), nothing jumps
and the Fang Gu lives. One extra Outsider is dealt. A jumped Outsider does not trigger their own death ability
(they did not die). Many Outsider claims hint at a Fang Gu; a Demon dying at night does too.

## Vigormortis
Each night but the first, the Vigormortis kills. A Minion it kills keeps their ability while dead and poisons one
Townsfolk neighbour, the nearest Townsfolk one way or the other round the circle (alive or dead), your choice: often
next to a troublesome Townsfolk. All of it holds only while the Vigormortis has its ability: if it dies, is drunk or
poisoned, or becomes something else, the dead Minions lose their abilities and the poison ends. A dead Minion who
becomes a non-Minion, or is drunk or poisoned, has no ability. One Outsider fewer is dealt (none removed if there are
none). Dead Minions still acting (a curse, madness, a transformation) are its fingerprint.

## No Dashii
Each night but the first, the No Dashii kills. The nearest Townsfolk each way round the circle, alive or dead, are
poisoned from the start (Outsiders and Minions are skipped), and the poison moves at once when seats change character
(a new No Dashii, a poisoned Townsfolk who becomes an Outsider) and ends if the No Dashii loses its ability. Its two
neighbours' information should mislead, in ways that fit what the evil players claim. Townsfolk who keep getting odd
information point to a No Dashii beside them.

## Vortox
Each night but the first, the Vortox kills. While it has its ability, every Townsfolk's information from their
ability is false, sober or not; choose false answers that mislead rather than ones the town can simply reverse, and
support evil's bluffs. A character or alignment change is not information and is always true; Outsiders' and
Minions' information is unaffected. At each dusk, if nobody was executed today (an execution of a dead player, or a
madness execution, counts), evil wins.

## Clockmaker
On the first night the Clockmaker learns how many steps it is from the Demon to its nearest Minion, counting seats
round the circle the shorter way, starting with the Demon's neighbour (next to each other is 1). The dead count as
seats; Minions are counted by character type, so a good Minion counts. A drunk or poisoned Clockmaker (a No Dashii
neighbour) should get a plausible wrong number, and under the Vortox it must be wrong. A player who becomes the
Clockmaker later learns the number at once. Evil often claims the Clockmaker on day one with a small number.

## Dreamer
Each night the Dreamer picks another player and sees two characters, one good and one evil, one of which is theirs.
For a Townsfolk or Outsider, show their character and a Minion or Demon (an in-play Minion misleads well); for a
Minion or Demon, show their character and a Townsfolk or Outsider. To help evil, show the good character the evil
player is claiming, or a quiet character such as the Snake Charmer, Sage, Mutant or Klutz; to help good, one nobody
claims. A Philosopher is shown as the Philosopher. Drunk, poisoned or under the Vortox, neither character is right.

## Snake Charmer
Each night the Snake Charmer picks a living player. Anyone but the Demon: nothing happens. The Demon: the two swap
characters and alignments at once; the old Demon becomes the good Snake Charmer and is poisoned for the rest of the
game; both are told what they now are. If both were evil (a Pit-Hag who made themself the Snake Charmer), both stay
evil. A drunk or poisoned Snake Charmer who picks the Demon swaps nothing, which the Mathematician detects. The swap
is not information: the Vortox doesn't touch it. The new Demon learns no Minions and may kill tonight.

## Mathematician
Each night the Mathematician learns how many players' abilities went wrong since dawn because of another
character: information that should have been true but was false (a poisoned No Dashii neighbour's, every Townsfolk's
under the Vortox), or an ability that should have worked but did not (a poisoned Demon's kill, a drunk Witch's cursed
nominator who lived, a poisoned Snake Charmer who picked the Demon). Being drunk or poisoned alone is not counted, nor
is a poisoned player's information that happened to be true, nor the Mathematician's own. Under the Vortox the number
itself is false. Usual answers are 0 or 1; a sudden jump suggests a new No Dashii.

## Flowergirl
Each night but the first, the Flowergirl learns whether a Demon voted today, for any nomination, whether or not
anyone was executed. Any Demon counts, even a dead one using their ghost vote, or one who has since changed character
(the Demon at the time of the vote is what counts). Drunk or poisoned, the answer may be wrong; under the Vortox it is
the opposite of the truth (a day with no votes gives a yes). The Flowergirl needs to track who voted.

## Town Crier
Each night but the first, the Town Crier learns whether any Minion nominated today (not who, nor how many). A Minion
is judged by character type when they nominated: a good Minion counts, an evil Townsfolk does not. Drunk or
poisoned, the answer may be wrong; under the Vortox it is the opposite (a day with no nominations gives a yes).
Minions who know a Town Crier is out let others nominate, so a steady no is weak evidence.

## Oracle
Each night but the first, after the Demon's kill, the Oracle learns how many dead players are evil by alignment: dead
Minions and Demons, Townsfolk or Outsiders turned evil, but not a good Minion or Demon. Drunk or poisoned, give a
plausible wrong count; under the Vortox any count but the true one. A dead evil Evil Twin counts; their good twin does
not. The Oracle tells the town whether its executions have been finding evil, and counters a Vigormortis.

## Seamstress
Once per game, at night, the Seamstress may pick two other players, alive or dead, and learns whether they are on
the same team (alignment, not character type: a good Witch and a Townsfolk are the same). They may decline as many
nights as they like; once they pick, the ability is gone, even if they were drunk or poisoned. Misinformed or under
the Vortox, the answer is wrong. The two Twins are always of different alignments.

## Sage
If the Demon's own attack kills the Sage at night, they wake and are shown two players, one of whom is the Demon that
killed them. Other deaths (the Witch, an execution, the Pit-Hag's arbitrary deaths) give nothing. Early in the game,
show two living players so evil keeps a chance; on the final night one may be dead. A drunk or poisoned Sage (a No
Dashii neighbour) is usually shown two players who are not that Demon; under the Vortox they never include it.

## Savant
Each day the Savant may visit you in private and hear two statements, one true and one false, without being told
which. Make both helpful and about this game, of the kind a Townsfolk ability would give (alignments of a pair, how
near the Demon sits to someone, how many are drunk or poisoned, whether a character is in play), and never simply
name the Demon. Give a false statement that is plausible for the setup's counts, often of the same form as the true
one, and don't repeat earlier ones. A drunk or poisoned Savant may hear two false (usually) or two true statements;
under the Vortox both are false. Anyone may visit you to bluff the Savant: the town sees only that they went.

## Philosopher
Once per game, at night, the Philosopher may choose a good character and gain its ability (they stay the
Philosopher). They then wake when that character wakes; a starting-knowledge ability acts at once. If that character
is in play, its player is drunk for as long as the Philosopher has their own ability: sober again if the Philosopher
dies, is drunk or poisoned, or changes character, and a player made into that character later is drunk too. Pit-Hags,
Dreamers and the Juggler see "Philosopher". A drunk Philosopher's choice is wasted.

## Artist
Once per game, by day, the Artist may visit you in private and ask any yes/no question. Answer honestly with yes, no
or "I don't know" (subjective questions, the future, intentions, or anything you cannot know, such as "Are we
winning?"); a question that is not yes/no can get "I don't know" too. A drunk or poisoned Artist may get a false
answer; under the Vortox an answer you could give must be false (a question like "is 2 + 2 four?" detects it). Evil
players may pretend to visit as the Artist: say nothing that gives them away.

## Juggler
On their first day as the Juggler (the first day of the game, or the day after becoming it), the Juggler publicly
guesses up to five players' characters. Count the correct guesses at once, against the true characters; that night
they learn the number. Drunk when guessing but sober at night: true. Drunk or poisoned at night: usually one off;
under the Vortox any other number. If they die before night, they learn nothing. Anyone may announce guesses to bluff
the Juggler; only the real one's count.

## Mutant
The Mutant must not try to convince anyone that they are an Outsider (any Outsider, not only the Mutant): claiming
it aloud, hinting or winking, confessing in a whisper you hear, telling you on a visit, or going silent when asked
all count. If they break it you may execute them, which is the day's execution and ends the day; you usually do for a
good player. A Mutant who claims a Townsfolk and plays it convincingly is safe. A drunk or poisoned Mutant (a
Sweetheart's drunk) may say anything.

## Sweetheart
When the Sweetheart dies, by any cause, choose one player to be drunk for the rest of the game. Almost always make a
living good Townsfolk drunk, ideally one whose information matters; if evil is winning convincingly, an Outsider, a
Minion or even the Demon can be made drunk instead (a drunk Demon kills nobody). A drunk or poisoned Sweetheart's death
does nothing. The drunk player is not told.

## Barber
If the Barber died today or tonight, that night the Demon may choose two players (alive or dead, themself included,
never another Demon) to swap characters. Alignments stay; each swapped player is told their new character, and a new
character's ability is fresh. The Demon may swap no one. With several Demons, you pick which one chooses. A drunk or
poisoned Barber's death gives no haircut, nor does a dead player who later becomes the Barber.

## Klutz
When the Klutz learns they died (at dawn after a night death, at once after an execution), they must publicly choose
an alive player; ask again until they name one. If the chosen player is evil, the Klutz's team loses at once (an evil
Klutz choosing an evil player makes good win); if good, nothing happens. Not information: the Vortox does not touch
it. A drunk or poisoned Klutz's choice does nothing.

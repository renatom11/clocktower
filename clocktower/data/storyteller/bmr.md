# Storyteller's notes: Bad Moon Rising

Compiled for the Storyteller judge from the Bad Moon Rising almanac's How to Run sections and examples, the
rulebook and the wiki, in our own words. One section per character: what the Storyteller decides for it, the
interactions to watch on this script, and how a good Storyteller handles them. Bad Moon Rising has many deaths and
many ways not to die: never say why someone lived or died. The judge reads the sections for the characters in play.

## Grandmother
At setup you pick the Grandchild: any good player, usually a Townsfolk (a Lunatic or a Goon is allowed and makes for
a strange first night). The Grandmother dies only when the Demon kills the Grandchild (the Pukka's poison death
counts), not to the Gambler, an execution or another night death, and not while she is drunk (the Sailor, the
Innkeeper, the Courtier). Both dying in one night can look like a Shabaloth or Po double kill. A Professor who
revives her lets her learn the Grandchild again. A drunk or poisoned Grandmother is best shown an evil player as
the good character they claim.

## Sailor
Each night the Sailor chooses a living player, and you decide who of the two is drunk until dusk: usually the
chosen player if a Townsfolk, usually the Sailor if an Outsider, Minion or Demon (so evil is not hobbled for free).
While sober the Sailor cannot die, even before they choose; a drunk Sailor dies to anything, including an execution
they asked for to prove themselves. A Sailor who made themself drunk is a good Gossip victim. Do not let the Sailor
learn from what you chose.

## Chambermaid
You give how many of the two chosen players woke tonight because of their own ability. Being woken to be told
something (the Demon learning the Exorcist, the Goon its new alignment) does not count; a Demon kept asleep by the
Exorcist does not count; a drunk player who woke still counts. The Assassin who chose no one still woke. A drunk
Chambermaid (the Goon) should get a number that misleads. Do not wake them when fewer than two others are alive.

## Exorcist
The Exorcist chooses a player each night but the first, never the same one twice running. A chosen Demon learns who
the Exorcist is and does not wake to act tonight: the Shabaloth and Po kill no one (a Po charge survives the night),
but the Pukka's earlier victim still dies. Choosing the Lunatic does nothing. When a night has no deaths for
reasons like this, a Tinker death or a Gossip kill can keep the town guessing, but never to give the Exorcist away.

## Innkeeper
Two chosen players cannot die tonight (only the Assassin gets through), and you choose which of them is drunk until
dusk: almost always a good player, depending on how the game is going; an Innkeeper who chose themself is usually
the drunk one. A drunk Innkeeper protects nobody. The Innkeeper stops a Pukka's delayed death and cures the poison.
A drunk Fool dies to the execution; a drunk Assassin's kill fails.

## Gambler
Each night but the first the Gambler guesses a player's character; a wrong guess kills them, a right one gives no
feedback. A guess at an evil bluff (the Devil's Advocate claiming Pacifist) is wrong. The death is their own
ability's, so protection stops it. Nothing to decide unless a character registers ambiguously.

## Gossip
You judge each day's first public statement: only definite, true statements about things you know count. Mumbled,
whispered, vague, future or intention statements do not. If true, a player of your choosing dies tonight (the
Gossip's sobriety at night decides). Pick a player who will actually die, not one protected or the Fool, so the
Gossip learns from it; this helps good and is more fun. Never pick to end the game by your own choice. A drunk-by-day
Gossip whose statement was true still kills at night if sober then.

## Courtier
Once per game the Courtier chooses a character: its player is drunk for three nights and three days. A drunk
Courtier spends the ability for nothing and is not woken again. Making the Demon drunk stops its kills for three
nights (the Shabaloth, the Po); making the Mastermind drunk lets an executed Demon end the game; a drunk Assassin's
kill fails, and a drunk Tinker, Grandmother or Moonchild loses their death effects. A Courtier choosing the Goon
becomes drunk and the Goon takes their alignment.

## Professor
Once per game the Professor chooses a dead player: a dead Townsfolk comes back to life at dawn (announced without
saying why). An Outsider, Minion or Demon, or a drunk Professor, does nothing, and the ability is gone. A revived
first-night character (the Grandmother) learns their information again at once. A player regurgitated by the
Shabaloth is no longer dead and cannot be chosen.

## Minstrel
When a Minion dies by execution, every other player (Travellers aside) is drunk until dusk tomorrow, so the Demon
cannot kill that night and information is unreliable. A Minion dying at night, or a dead Minion executed, does not
trigger it. A drunk Devil's Advocate's saved Minion dies and triggers it again. An executed drunk Zombuul dies for
real and good wins. Give drunk information that misleads, as with any drunk player.

## Tea Lady
While both her alive neighbours are good, they cannot die (only the Assassin gets through). Recheck as players die
or change alignment (the Goon). The Tea Lady herself is not protected. When choosing who dies to the Gossip or
Moonchild, avoid a neighbour she protects (it would not kill).

## Pacifist
When a good player is executed, you decide whether they die: about once a game is usual, more likely at a critical
moment near the end, less if good is already winning; rarely never at all, to make the Pacifist look suspicious. A
drunk Pacifist saves nobody (an Innkeeper's self-drink, the Minstrel). Do not say why a player survived.

## Fool
The first time the Fool would die, they don't (once); a drunk or poisoned Fool dies. Protection that saves them first
(the Tea Lady, the Innkeeper) keeps the ability. The Assassin kills them anyway. The Gossip's victim should not be a
Fool with their ability.

## Goon
The first player each night to choose the Goon with their ability is drunk until dusk, and the Goon takes their
alignment (told with a thumbs up or down). A Demon choosing the Goon first fails its kills that night (the
Shabaloth's and Po's later picks too). A sober Assassin's kill on the Goon still lands. Remember the alignment when
judging the Tea Lady, the Moonchild and who wins.

## Lunatic
At setup you build the illusion: the Lunatic believes they are a Demon (usually the one in play, sometimes another),
sees any players as their Minions (sometimes the real Demon among them) and any three good characters as bluffs,
even ones in play. The real Demon learns who the Lunatic is and what they choose each night, and may kill the same
players to keep the illusion. The Lunatic wakes as their believed Demon would (a Lunatic-Zombuul rarely). Their
clues should be subtle: the players they attack do not die.

## Tinker
You may kill the Tinker at any time. It is most interesting at night, when the town cannot tell why they died; by
day it is announced at once. Never when it would end the game; sometimes never at all, to make the Tinker look
suspicious. A drunk or protected Tinker does not die. A Tinker death can cover a night with no Demon kill (the
Exorcist) or mimic another Demon's pattern, but use this sparingly and to keep the game close.

## Moonchild
When the Moonchild learns they died, they publicly choose a living player; a good one dies tonight. The Moonchild's
sobriety at night decides. Nothing to decide, but play along with an evil player bluffing a Moonchild's curse, and
privately prompt a Moonchild who forgets to choose.

## Godfather
At setup the Godfather means one more or one fewer Outsider. On the first night they learn which Outsiders are in
play; a drunk or poisoned Godfather may be shown a false set of a size the setup allows. After an Outsider's day
death they kill a player that night (one kill however many died). A protected Tinker who survives an execution does
not trigger it; the Tinker dying by day does.

## Devil's Advocate
Each night they choose a living player (not last night's) who, if executed tomorrow, does not die. Only while the
Devil's Advocate keeps a working ability: a drunk one's choice fails, and the Minstrel then fires on the Minion's
death. Do not say why an executed player lived. A Mastermind's extra day still ends on any execution, saved or not.

## Assassin
Once per game, at night after the first, they kill a player despite everything: protection, the Fool, the Sailor,
the Tea Lady, the Zombuul's first death. A drunk or poisoned Assassin's choice fails and the ability is spent. A
sober Assassin choosing the Goon is made drunk but the kill lands.

## Mastermind
If the Demon dies by execution and the game would end, play one more day without saying the Demon died; that day's
execution decides: a good player executed, evil wins; an evil player or nobody, good wins. A Zombuul's first,
seeming death does not trigger it. A drunk Mastermind (the Courtier, the Minstrel) does nothing, and the game ends.

## Zombuul
The Zombuul kills only on nights after a day when nobody died. The first time it dies it lives on but registers as
dead (it may still vote with its ghost vote and is shown as dead); a drunk Zombuul dies for real. Executing it again
kills it. With the Zombuul seemingly dead, the town can see two alive while evil still has a living Demon.

## Pukka
Each night the Pukka poisons a player, who dies the next night (still poisoned) and is then healthy. A drunk Pukka
poisons nobody, and the earlier victim waits. The Exorcist stops the new poison but not the old victim's death; the
Innkeeper stops the death and cures the poison. The poisoned player's information should be false and misleading,
as with any poison.

## Shabaloth
Each night after the first the Shabaloth kills two players. Just before it acts, you may regurgitate one of the dead
players it chose last night (alive again at dawn): rarely, once a game or maybe twice, usually a good player, a
Minion only rarely, especially when evil is struggling. A Goon chosen first makes it drunk, so the second kill
fails. Regurgitating a Moonchild gives them another curse later.

## Po
The Po may choose no one; next time it chooses three. A drunk Po's "no one" still charges; the first night and an
exorcised night are not choices and keep the charge. A poisoned Po's three picks all fail. A Goon among the three
stops the later ones. Nights with no deaths followed by three are the Po's tell; a Tinker death can muddy it.

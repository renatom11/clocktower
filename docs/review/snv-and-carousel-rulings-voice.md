## Part A (Sects & Violets): Group 1: the core, the Minions, the Evil Twin and the Demons (rulings 1 to 21)

**S1.1. Madness: what the Storyteller hears. This is the most important ruling; take it slowly.** When the
Storyteller decides whether a mad player broke their madness today, it looks at what that player said aloud, their
nominations and defences, and the character abilities they publicly claimed, from the moment they were made mad,
never anyone's private thoughts. The scripted Storyteller hears only what was said aloud, as a human Storyteller
would. But a Storyteller judge, by default, also hears every whisper. So under the default, a mad player who whispers
"I'm actually mad" to one friend is overheard, and that confession is fatal: they can be executed for it, as in the
almanac's Flowergirl example. The judge can be set to hear only public speech, but that is not the default. Question:
when judging madness, should the Storyteller hear whispers? Options: one, as now, the judge hears whispers by default;
two, never whispers, for any judge; three, a random share of whispers is overheard, so a private confession is a risk
rather than certain death. Engine now: option one. What follows: the wiki's advice to the Mutant, to come out secretly
to one or two trusted players, is always fatal, and the players are told to assume the Storyteller hears everything,
whispers included. Reporting a mad player to the Storyteller in private is possible only by visiting the Storyteller,
which comes up in group 3.

**S1.2. Madness: the minimum test.** The Cerenovus makes a player mad about being a good character. Engine now: the
scripted Storyteller and the simple rules-based judge say the player broke madness unless they claimed that character
aloud, it is still the latest character they claimed, and they never said they aren't it or that they're mad, like "I
am mad" or "the Cerenovus picked me". Saying "I'm not mad" is fine, as in the almanac's Barber example. For the
Mutant, who must not be mad about being an Outsider, claiming an Outsider or saying they're mad breaks it. This is
only a floor: a model judge also weighs effort, hints and how the story builds. Question: should the scripted
Storyteller sometimes be lenient, at a set chance, since it can't judge effort?

**S1.3. Madness: when it is judged, and the day of a madness execution.** Engine now: the Cerenovus's target is judged
once a day, after private conversations and the town square, before nominations open. An execution for madness is the
day's one execution, and the day goes straight to night with no nominations, as the almanac says. What they say
during nominations doesn't count. Question: is once, before nominations, right? Options: also judge at dusk, seeing
nominations and defences, though someone may already have been executed, and the almanac also allows executing them
the next day; or judge after every part of the day, closer to "at any time" but many more judge calls.

**S1.4. Madness: "might be executed".** Engine now: a good player who broke madness is executed nine times in ten,
because the rulebook says it's the player's choice even when it ends the game; an evil one half the time, because the
almanac says "might" lets the Storyteller stop evil using madness as a strategy. The rules-based judge always executes
a good one and decides an evil one by how the game stands. A drunk or poisoned Cerenovus makes nobody mad, and neither
does one who loses their ability before the judgment, for example by dying, unless the Vigormortis killed them.
Question: is that right?

**S1.5. Character changes.** When a player's character changes, engine now: they're told at once, in private, their
new character and ability, and whether they're now evil or still good. This is never false, even under the Vortox or
when drunk or poisoned. The new ability starts fresh, even a once-per-game one an earlier holder used, following the
Barber's almanac entry. The old character's ongoing effects end, so a poisoner who changes stops poisoning. A player
who becomes a Demon isn't told the Minions, and the Minions aren't told. A new Evil Twin is paired, and an Evil Twin
whose twin changed side is dealt with. If the new character's night step is still to come tonight, they act tonight.
Question: is that right?

**S1.6. Dead players keeping their ability.** The Vigormortis's dead Minions keep their ability. Engine now: it's kept
as a Minion ability, so a dead Minion who becomes another Minion keeps it, and one who becomes a Townsfolk doesn't.
While it's kept they wake at their step, the ability works and its effects carry on; once lost, the effects end. If
the Vigormortis is drunk or poisoned, its Minions have no ability. Question: is that right?

**S1.7. Poison worked out fresh.** The No Dashii's and the Vigormortis's poison isn't placed once; it's worked out
again whenever someone's sobriety matters, so it follows every change at once. A poison never touches its own source:
the No Dashii isn't poisoned by its own poison. Question: is that right?

**S1.8. Information that must be false.** Under the Vortox, Townsfolk information must be false. Engine now: no
scripted choice or judge can give a true answer. Whether it counts as Townsfolk information goes by the character the
player believes they are. If no false answer exists, the true one is given, since there is nothing false to give. It
also works for Trouble Brewing information roles, so a custom script mixing them works. Question: is that right,
especially giving the true answer when nothing false exists?

**S1.9. Abilities set off by someone else's nomination or execution.** For example, the Evil Twin's when the good twin
is executed. Engine now: the nominee's own reaction, like the Virgin's, comes first, then everyone else's. An
execution counts whether or not the player dies, so executing a dead player counts too. Question: is that right?

**S1.10. Setup changes from the Demon.** The Fang Gu adds an Outsider and the Vigormortis removes one. Engine now:
these count. Removing an Outsider when there are none removes nothing and adds no extra Townsfolk. Bad Moon Rising's
Godfather keeps its old dealing, so those games are unchanged, though its setup check now also accepts that lower
count, which the official rule allows. Question: is that right?

**S1.11. The Witch.** Engine now: only a sober, healthy Witch makes a curse. The curse pauses while the Witch is drunk
or poisoned and comes back when she sobers, as in the rulebook's example; it follows the player through a character
change, and ends when the Witch loses her ability, unless the Vigormortis killed her. With three players alive, the
Witch loses her ability at once, a curse already made included, and isn't woken. A cursed player's death is announced
at once, never why. Question: is that right?

**S1.12. The Cerenovus.** It may choose any player, alive or dead, itself too, and any good character on the script.
Question: when the Cerenovus is drunk or poisoned, is the target still told they're mad? Engine now: yes, so nothing
gives the wasted ability away, but nobody is executed for it. Option: tell them nothing.

**S1.13. The Pit-Hag: what "in play" means.** Engine now: a character is in play if any player has it, alive or dead;
the Drunk's apparent Townsfolk doesn't count. A dead player can be changed, and the Pit-Hag may change themself.
Question: is that right?

**S1.14. The Pit-Hag: deaths when a Demon is made.** The almanac lets deaths that night be arbitrary. Engine now: at
the Pit-Hag's step, the Storyteller decides up to two extra deaths and whether the Demons' attacks still kill. The
scripted Storyteller follows the almanac: a Demon made into another Demon, nobody dies four times in five; a new evil
Demon, the old Demon dies four times in five and the new one attacks as usual; a good Demon made on the final night,
four or fewer players alive, the evil Demon dies four times in five; otherwise, as usual. The rules-based judge has no
preference. Question: is that right? Options: let the Storyteller protect only chosen players, or allow more than two
deaths.

**S1.15. The Evil Twin's twin.** Engine now: any good player at setup, picked at random. Both always learn each other
truly, even if the Evil Twin is drunk on the first night, because it's knowledge of each other, like a character
change. Option: a drunk Evil Twin is shown a false pair.

**S1.16. The Evil Twin's win conditions.** "If the good player is executed, evil wins": engine now, whichever of the
two is good, even a good Evil Twin, executed alive or dead, while the Evil Twin has their ability, meaning alive and
sober, or dead with the ability kept by the Vigormortis. "Good can't win while you both live": both alive and the Evil
Twin sober. Question: is that right?

**S1.17. Re-pairing the twins.** Engine now: if the twins end up on the same side, for example the Fang Gu jumps into
the twin, the Storyteller picks a new twin of the other alignment, and both are told. A new Evil Twin made by the
Pit-Hag is paired at once; on the final night the twin may be the Demon or a dead player, if they're of the other
alignment. Question: is that right?

**S1.18. The Fang Gu's jump.** Engine now: the first Outsider it would kill becomes an evil Fang Gu and is told, then
the old Fang Gu dies. An Outsider who wouldn't die, already dead or protected, doesn't jump. It happens once a game,
remembered whatever happens to the Fang Gu. The new Fang Gu isn't told the Minions. Not handled: the Scarlet Woman
jinx, a custom-script case, where the Scarlet Woman would take over instead. Question: is that right?

**S1.19. The Vigormortis's poison.** It poisons a Townsfolk neighbour of each Minion it kills. Engine now: at the
kill, the Storyteller picks a side at random, and the nearest Townsfolk that way, alive or dead, is poisoned, worked
out again as things change, so one Townsfolk per Minion is always poisoned. Option: fix the player at the kill. Second
question: a dead Minion who is drunk or poisoned loses their own ability, but their poison goes on, because the
poison is the Vigormortis's ability. Option: it stops too. A kill that fails, on a protected Minion, keeps nothing.

**S1.20. The No Dashii.** Engine now: it poisons its nearest Townsfolk by character type, so an evil Townsfolk
counts, from the start of the game, whether the No Dashii is alive or dead. With only one Townsfolk in play, only that
one. Question: is that right?

**S1.21. The Vortox.** Engine now: every Townsfolk's information is false while it has its ability, so not while dead,
drunk or poisoned. At dusk, if nobody was executed today, evil wins. Executing a dead player counts, and so does a
madness execution; a Witch death doesn't. Question: is that right?

## Part A (Sects & Violets): Group 2: the information Townsfolk, and the Snake Charmer (rulings 22 to 33)

(Say once at the start of this group: every information result can be false for a drunk or poisoned player, is
always false under the Vortox, and a false result always fits some possible setup.)

**S2.1. What the Mathematician counts.** The Mathematician learns how many players' abilities worked abnormally
because of another character. Engine now counts: any information a working ability couldn't have given, so a drunk or
poisoned player's false result, and every false result under the Vortox, as the almanac does; a poisoned player who
happens to get true information doesn't count, as in the almanac's Empath example. It also counts abilities that
would have acted but whose holder was drunk or poisoned: the Demon's attack, the Witch's curse, including a cursed
nominator who lives, the Cerenovus's madness, the Pit-Hag's change when it would have worked, the Snake Charmer
choosing the Demon, and the Evil Twin when the good twin is executed. Not counted now: a kill stopped by protection,
which can't happen on this script; the Demon kills the Pit-Hag stops; a drunk or poisoned No Dashii or Vortox, whose
effects are continuous; the Recluse registering as evil; and the Drunk's own false results, which the official jinx
counts on scripts with both. Question: should any of those be counted?

**S2.2. The Mathematician's "since dawn".** Engine now: each night it counts players with an abnormal result from the
previous day or that night, and on the first night since the game began, each player once. Its own failures aren't
counted, since it doesn't detect its own ability failing, but another Mathematician's would be. Question: is that
right?

**S2.3. Votes and nominations as they happened.** Engine now: the Flowergirl and Town Crier go by each player's
character at the moment they voted or nominated, so a Demon who voted and was then swapped by the Snake Charmer that
night still counts as a Demon voting. A dead Demon's ghost vote counts. Question: is that right?

**S2.4. The judge and the Dreamer's bluff.** Engine now: when the Dreamer is shown a pair of characters, the
rules-based judge prefers a pair including the character the evil player claimed, as it already did for single
characters. Question: is that right?

**S2.5. The Clockmaker.** Engine now: it counts steps the short way round the circle, dead players included, from any
Demon to the nearest Minion by character type, so a good Minion made by the Pit-Hag counts. A false number is
anything from one to half the table. Question: a player who becomes the Clockmaker after the first night learns the
number at once, following the Barber's almanac entry. Options: at the next night, or never.

**S2.6. The Dreamer.** Engine now: it goes by character type, not alignment, so a good Witch is shown as the Witch
plus a good character. The scripted false character: for a good player, a Minion in play half the time, since the wiki
says almost always, otherwise any Minion or Demon. For an evil player, half the time the good character they last
claimed aloud, as the almanac suggests to help evil; otherwise a secretive good character three times in ten, like
the Snake Charmer, Sage, Mutant or Klutz; otherwise another, never the Dreamer itself. Misinformed or under the
Vortox, neither shown character is right. The scripted Storyteller only counts claims made aloud. Question: is that
right?

**S2.7. The Snake Charmer.** Engine now: it chooses living players only; choosing themself or a non-Demon does
nothing. Choosing the Demon, by character, a good Demon too, swaps characters and alignments, and both are told at
once. The old Demon is then poisoned for the rest of the game, even through later character changes, reading
"permanently" literally. Option: the poison ends once they stop being the Snake Charmer. The new Demon isn't told the
Minions and acts tonight. Question: is that right? (A charmed Vigormortis is a later ruling.)

**S2.8. The Mathematician's false number.** Engine now: misinformed, one or two off the truth; under the Vortox, any
number but the truth. Question: is that right?

**S2.9. The Flowergirl and the Town Crier.** Engine now: a yes or no about the day just past, not on the first night;
misinformed, the opposite. A day ended by a madness execution, with no nominations, gives no, or yes under the Vortox,
as in its almanac example. Question: is that right?

**S2.10. The Oracle.** Engine now: it counts dead players who are evil, at its step after the Demon's kill, so a
dead good Evil Twin isn't evil, and a Townsfolk turned evil is. Question: is that right?

**S2.11. The Seamstress.** Engine now: she may decline any night, and naming only one player counts as declining.
The ability is spent once two players are chosen, even if she's drunk or poisoned. Dead players may be chosen.
Question: is naming one player the same as declining?

**S2.12. The Sage.** Engine now: the Sage learns only if the Demon's own attack killed them tonight; the Vigormortis's
and Fang Gu's attacks count, but not the Witch, the Pit-Hag's extra deaths or an execution. They're shown that Demon
and another player; before the final night the other is alive nine times in ten, on the final night anyone.
Misinformed, two players who aren't that Demon, one dead and one alive on the final night, as in the almanac's drunk
Sage. Their sobriety at their step decides. Question: is that right?

## Part A (Sects & Violets): Group 3: visits to the Storyteller, the last Townsfolk, and the Outsiders (rulings 34 to 48; madness first)

**S3.3. Madness: what's said on a visit counts.** Players can now visit the Storyteller in private. Engine now: what a
player says there is madness evidence, heard by the Storyteller whatever the hearing setting, but it isn't "aloud",
so it doesn't count as claiming a character for the Cerenovus. This is the first way to report someone to the
Storyteller: a model judge reads everything said on every visit. The scripted Storyteller only checks the visitor's
own words about themself. Question: is that right?

**S3.6. Madness: when the Mutant is judged.** Engine now: twice a day. Once before nominations, on the day's talk, as
for the Cerenovus, and again at dusk, on what was said since, nominations and defences, if nobody was executed today.
Nothing is judged twice, and the "might be executed" chance is rolled each time. Never at night, since nobody talks.
Option: after every part of the day, closer to "at any time"; a madness execution would then end the day earlier.

**S3.11. Madness: the Mutant.** Engine now: the Mutant must not claim any Outsider, or just "an Outsider", aloud, in
a whisper the Storyteller hears, or on a visit, and must not say they're mad. They're judged at those two points and
executed by the usual chances, nine in ten if good, half if evil. Question: is that right?

**S3.1. The charmed Vigormortis.** This changes the earlier Snake Charmer ruling. Engine now: the dead Minions'
abilities and their poison belong to the Vigormortis character, not its player. When the Snake Charmer charms it, or
the Barber swaps it, they move to the new Vigormortis, whose sobriety then decides them; the old one, now the poisoned
Snake Charmer, keeps nothing. Option: they end, as before. Question: is that right?

**S3.2. Visiting the Storyteller.** On a private-time turn, any player may visit the Storyteller claiming a visiting
ability, the Savant's or the Artist's, with the Artist's question. The town sees who went, never why. A player with
the ability, or who believes they have it, is answered in private; anyone else is told the Storyteller listened and
nodded, which helps evil bluff, as the almanac suggests. Engine now: one visit a day per player, and a visit is that
turn's only conversation, so a whisper sent with it isn't sent. Options: allow a whisper alongside it, or more than
one visit a day. Dead players may visit, but are answered only if their ability works while dead, which none of
these do. Trouble Brewing and Bad Moon Rising are unchanged. Question: is that right?

**S3.4. Abilities gained from another character, the Philosopher.** Engine now: the player is woken at that
character's step as that character, and both the Philosopher and the drunk original act. A player whose character
changes loses the abilities gained through the old one; the almanac says the duplicate sobers up if the Philosopher
becomes a different character. Once-per-game abilities are spent per player, so the Philosopher and the original
each have theirs, and a player who becomes that character later starts fresh. Question: is that right?

**S3.5. How the Juggler guesses.** Engine now: the Juggler's guesses are a list version of the shared public-ability
move, up to five guesses of a player and a character, heard by the town, and offered only on scripts with such a
character. Question: is that right?

**S3.7. The Savant's statements.** Engine now: twelve kinds of statement, checked by code. For example, two players
are the same alignment; the Demon sits within one or two seats of a living player; exactly so many living players are
drunk or poisoned; a living player was chosen at night by someone's ability; a given character is in play; exactly so
many Outsiders; so many of the Savant's living neighbours are evil; a living player is evil; a Minion sits next to
the Demon; and from day two, everyone got true information last night, a Demon voted yesterday, a Minion nominated
yesterday, so many dead players are evil. The scripted Storyteller picks the true statement's kind at random,
favouring ones about living players, never a true "this player is evil" about the Demon, and nothing already said;
the false one is the same kind half the time. Drunk or poisoned, both false seven times in ten, otherwise both true;
under the Vortox, both false. The rules-based judge has no preference, and a model judge ranks a shortlist. Question:
is that right? Options: more kinds, like seat ranges or claims; a chance to favour statements about today's claims.

**S3.8. The Philosopher.** Engine now: they choose any Townsfolk or Outsider, or none, keeping the ability. It's spent
even if they're drunk or poisoned, which the Mathematician counts. The player who has the chosen character is drunk
while the Philosopher has their ability, including a player made that character later. A "you start knowing" ability
gained after the first night acts at once; on the first night, at its step. Choosing the Philosopher does nothing.
Question: is that right?

**S3.9. The Artist.** Engine now: simple questions are answered by code, like "is this player that character, evil,
or an Outsider", "is the Demon one of these three", "are these two the same alignment", or "is this character in
play"; misinformed or under the Vortox, the opposite. Anything else goes to the judge, who says yes, no, or "I don't
know"; the scripted Storyteller says "I don't know". The main question: a question that isn't yes or no gets "I don't
know" and spends the ability. The almanac says to ask them to rephrase. Option: the judge can say "ask again" without
spending it. An empty question isn't a use.

**S3.10. The Juggler's count.** Engine now: only a living Juggler's first claim with at least one guess, on their
first day as the Juggler, counts, drunk or not. Correct guesses are counted at once against true characters; that
night's sobriety decides what they learn, one off if misinformed, any other number under the Vortox. Question: a
claim with no guesses isn't a use. Option: it is, and they learn zero.

**S3.12. The Sweetheart.** Engine now: any death counts, and their sobriety as they die decides, so a drunk or
poisoned Sweetheart's death is wasted. The Storyteller makes another player drunk for the rest of the game, through
later changes. The scripted Storyteller picks a living good Townsfolk nine times in ten, otherwise any other living
player; the rules-based judge picks an evil player when evil is well ahead, otherwise a good Townsfolk. It's decided at
once. Question: is that right?

**S3.13. The Barber.** Engine now: a death by day gives haircuts the coming night, a death at night that night, and
the Barber's sobriety as they die decides. The Demon, picked at random if there are several, may choose two players,
alive or dead, themself included, never another Demon, or nobody; naming one counts as nobody. The swap keeps
alignments, and each is told. Question: is that right?

**S3.14. The Klutz.** Engine now: the Klutz chooses as soon as they learn they died, at dawn or at once by day,
publicly, and must choose, asked again until the choice is legal. If they choose an evil player, their team loses at
once, so an evil Klutz choosing evil makes good win. Their sobriety at the choice decides, and the Vortox doesn't
affect it. Anyone can fake a Klutz choice as a public claim; it does nothing. Question: is that right?

**S3.15. The script plays end to end.** Sects & Violets now loads and plays at every size, with and without the
rules-based judge. The players' instructions cover madness and what the Storyteller hears, character and alignment
changes, the Vortox, visits and the Juggler's guesses. They tell players to assume the Storyteller hears everything,
whispers included, which is true by default and safe otherwise. Question: is that the right advice to give players?

## Part A (Sects & Violets): Optional extras (ask whether they want to continue)

### The Storyteller's weights

How often the scripted Storyteller does something, when no judge is used. For each, say the number in plain words, for
example "a quarter of the time", and ask whether it feels right.

**SW.1. mad_execute_good**: a good player who broke madness is executed nine times in ten.
**SW.2. mad_execute_evil**: an evil player who broke madness is executed half the time.
**SW.3. pithag_quiet**: when the Pit-Hag turns a Demon into another Demon, nobody dies that night four times in five.
**SW.4. pithag_kill_old_demon**: when the Pit-Hag makes a new evil Demon, the old Demon dies four times in five.
**SW.5. pithag_final_kill**: when the Pit-Hag makes a good Demon on the final night, the evil Demon dies four times in
five.
**SW.6. final_night_alive**: a night counts as the final night when four or fewer players are alive at dusk (a count,
not a chance).
**SW.7. dreamer_minion_in_play**: a good player is shown to the Dreamer with a Minion in play half the time.
**SW.8. dreamer_bluff**: an evil player is shown as the good character they claimed aloud half the time.
**SW.9. dreamer_secretive**: otherwise, as a secretive good character three times in ten.
**SW.10. sage_alive**: before the final night, the Sage's second player is alive nine times in ten.
**SW.11. savant_same_kind**: the Savant's false statement is the same kind as the true one half the time.
**SW.12. savant_both_false**: a drunk or poisoned Savant gets two false statements seven times in ten.
**SW.13. sweetheart_townsfolk**: the Sweetheart's death makes a living good Townsfolk drunk nine times in ten.

### Three questions about the Storyteller judge

**J.1. The Pit-Hag's deaths.** The rules-based judge has no preference about deaths when the Pit-Hag makes a Demon, so
the scripted style decides. Should it lean by the balance of the game, for example killing the old Demon less often
when evil is behind?
**J.2. The Savant.** The rules-based judge has no preference among the thousands of possible statement pairs, and a

## Part B (experimental characters): Group 1: cross-cutting engine decisions (items 1 to 12)

**C1.1. Who hears what: whispers and the characters that listen to speech (rulings 120 and 123). The most important
cross-cutting ruling; take it slowly.** Two new characters react to what players say. The Mezepheles, a Minion, has a
secret word: the first good player to say it turns evil that night. The Yaggababble, a Demon, has a secret phrase:
each time it says it publicly in a day, a player might die that night. Engine now: everything a player says to other
players reaches these abilities. The town square, accusations, defences, comments, the remark made with a vote and the
words of a public ability claim are said aloud; whispers are said in private. What a player tells the Storyteller on a
visit, and night choices, never count. The Mezepheles counts whispers, because its text just says "say", and the
engine's Storyteller is taken to hear every whisper. The Yaggababble counts only what it says aloud, as its text says
"publicly". Question: should the Mezepheles hear whispers? Options: one, as now; two, public speech only for both,
since a human Storyteller doesn't hear whispers; three, also count what is said on a visit. What follows from now: a
good player who whispers the word to one friend turns evil exactly as if they had said it to the town. Note for the
owner: on Sects & Violets the scripted Storyteller judges madness only on what was said aloud, and only a judge hears
whispers by default; here the scripted Storyteller hears whispers for the Mezepheles too.

**C1.2. When a word counts as said, and near misses (rulings 121 and 122).** Engine now: the word or phrase counts when
its words appear together, in order, as whole words, ignoring case and punctuation; an apostrophe joins its word, so
"let's" is "lets". "Trusted" is not "trust", and "distrust" is not "trust". Each separate occurrence counts, so saying
the phrase twice in one sentence is twice for the Yaggababble. A near miss, such as a plural, another ending, or a
one-letter slip, is never counted by the scripted Storyteller. With a judge, each near miss is a judged call: the
rules-based judge counts another form of the same word, like an added "s", "ed", "ing" or "ly", but not a misspelling
or an inserted word; a model judge decides as it sees fit. A near miss ruled in counts from when it was said, so the
first speaker stays the first. Question: is strict matching right for the scripted Storyteller? Options: also count
other endings of the same word for the scripted Storyteller; never count near misses, even with a judge.

**C1.3. When a jinx is in effect (ruling 4).** Engine now: an official jinx applies whenever both its characters are
on the script, in play or not, following the official rule that jinxes belong to the script. The code then checks the
situation the jinx describes, such as both in play or who chose whom. Trouble Brewing, Bad Moon Rising and Sects &
Violets hold no official jinx, so their play is unchanged. Option: apply a jinx only when both characters are in play;
of the base-script jinxes, that changes only the Chambermaid's with the Mathematician. Question: keep "on the script"?

**C1.4. Homebrew characters (ruling 2).** A custom script may contain a homebrew character: an entry with a name and a
team that isn't in the official data. Engine now: a homebrew Townsfolk, Outsider, Minion or Demon is never built, even
when it has an official character's name and text. For example a corpus script's Witch Hunt Witch is the Witch with
"several in the bag", so the script reports that character as not implemented yet. Options: play a homebrew entry
whose name and ability text match an official character as that character, so the Witch Hunt Witch would load without
its bag rule; or build homebrew characters case by case. Why: a homebrew entry can change rules its text doesn't
show, through special fields or house rules, so playing it as the official one would be a silent ruling.

**C1.5. Travellers, Fabled and Loric (ruling 3).** Engine now: they stay on the script by name and are listed on the
character sheet as not in play in this engine, but they are never dealt, never woken, and their rules are not applied,
including the Djinn's and the Bootlegger's house rules. Setup counts are the normal ones, and the players are told
plainly that they are absent. Options: refuse such scripts; build Travellers and Fabled, which needs a design agreed
first for exile and the Storyteller's helpers. Why: most corpus scripts list a few, and the four player types alone
make a legal game. Question: is playing without them acceptable for the benchmark?

**C1.6. The player prompt for custom scripts (rulings 12 and 125).** Engine now: any script other than the three base
scripts gets the Trouble Brewing prompt with generic sentences: the script's name, its character sheet with jinxes and
the characters not in play, its Demons named in the team sizes, a generic night order ("protect, poison or make drunk
before the Demon, most information after"), that character and alignment changes are told at once, and that
information is true unless an ability says otherwise. The Sects & Violets madness sentences appear only when a
character's ability says "mad", the visit sentences only when a character visits the Storyteller, and a sentence on
listening abilities only when one is on the script: it says such abilities exist, how a word counts, and that only
the holder learns the word. The Trouble Brewing prompt is unchanged byte for byte. Options: build the night-order
sentence from the script's own order; describe each script's setup modifiers. This is a draft for review, as the Bad
Moon Rising and Sects & Violets prompts were.

**C1.7. Secret votes (rulings 74, 116 and 120).** The Organ Grinder, while alive and working, makes every vote eyes
closed. Engine now: each voter sees only their own hand, the prompt gives no tally, nominations don't say who is about
to die, and the count goes only to the Storyteller's record; only whether someone is executed is announced at the
end. The remark a player makes with a secret vote counts as private speech. With the Butler jinx, the Butler isn't
shown the master's hand and may raise theirs, but it counts only if the master's is up. The Vizier, who may execute
straight after a vote in which a good player voted, is still asked under secret votes, because the Storyteller knows
who voted. Question: is that right? Options: never ask the Vizier under secret votes; announce the final count.

**C1.8. Setup changes belong to the character really in play (rulings 71, 83, 87, 101, 53 and 84).** Several new

**C1.11. Abilities gained from another character that the engine can't fully pass on (rulings 47, 48, 107 and 136).**

**C1.12. Confirm these in one go: loading and playability (rulings 1, 13, 37, 38, 39, 64, 81, 99, 119, 126, 137 and 138,

Run this group straight after group 4, before the optional weights. These five characters were kept back for a design
with the owner, then built on one condition: each one can always be taken out again. Each character lives in its own
section of the code, with its own tests, notes, test script and rulings, so it can be switched off with one setting,
or deleted for good by removing its section and a short list of its files; either way the others and every recorded
replay stay as they were, and both ways are tested for each character. So for each character, the first
item asks whether to keep it at all, and the items after it are its real decisions. If the owner says to remove a
character, record that and skip the rest of that character's items, unless the owner wants to rule on them in case it
comes back.

### Lil' Monsta (items 81 to 84)

**C6.1. Keep Lil' Monsta at all? (rulings 139 to 150; switch: lilmonsta)** Lil' Monsta is a Demon that no player is
dealt: the Minions pass its token among themselves each night, and whoever babysits it is the Demon while keeping their
own Minion ability. Engine now: built as a token the babysitter carries beside their own Minion character, which is
the first option of group 4's item 69, with all seven of its official jinxes. It makes three more corpus scripts play
end to end. Question: keep it, or remove it? Options: one, keep it as built; two, remove it; three,
keep it but rebuild it as a character passed between the Minions, simpler but losing the babysitter's own ability,
which the text keeps. Why it was built this way: the text says the babysitter keeps their ability, and only a token
can say that.

**C6.2. Who the babysitter registers as (ruling 143).** The babysitter is a Minion carrying a Demon's token. Engine now:
they register both as Lil' Monsta and as their own Minion character, so information may show either; a Spy babysitter
can also register as good characters, as the Spy does. Two abilities are fixed: the Fortune Teller always sees a Demon
there, and the Slayer's shot always kills them, because the babysitter "is the Demon". Every other ability that asks
whether a player registers as the Demon treats the babysitter as able to go either way, the Storyteller's call.
Question: is "either way" right for those other abilities? Options: one, as now; two, always the Demon to every
ability. Why not two: the engine's registration can't say "always the Demon, and also their Minion" at once, so
"always the Demon" would mean losing the Minion side everywhere.

**C6.3. A player made Lil' Monsta when every Minion character is taken (ruling 146 and U14).** When the Pit-Hag, the
Engineer, the Summoner or a tea party makes a player Lil' Monsta, that player also becomes a Minion character of
their choice, one no living player has, and babysits the token; a good player made it stays good. The fuzzer found
that at 13 to 15 players with all the script's Minions alive and in play, there is no free Minion character, and the
engine then gives them a second copy of one. That happened 3 times in 1,500 forced games. Question: what should happen
then? Options: one, as now, a second copy; two, the change fails, reading the Pit-Hag's "if not in play" as also
needing a free Minion character; three, the player keeps their own character and simply carries the token. The
suggested rulings are two or three; the engine does one only because something had to happen.

**C6.4. Confirm these in one go: Lil' Monsta's other rules (rulings 139, 141, 142, 144, 145, 147, 148, 149 and 150).**
- Ruling 139: with Lil' Monsta in play no Demon is dealt; a Minion takes the Demon's seat, so there is one Minion
  more than the table's, and Outsiders follow the other Minions' setup changes.
- Ruling 141: each night, the first included, every living Minion who knows they are one names a babysitter; if they
  agree, that one babysits, if not the Storyteller picks among those named. Everyone involved learns who babysits.
- Ruling 142: the Minions learn each other and that nobody is the Demon yet; they learn the bluffs at the first-night
  step; the custom prompt explains how Lil' Monsta works.
- Ruling 144: each night but the first, the Storyteller may kill any living player but the babysitter, or nobody. It
  is the Demon's kill, so protections stop it; a drunk or poisoned babysitter kills nobody.
- Ruling 145: Lil' Monsta dies with its babysitter, however they die, and good wins unless the Scarlet Woman jinx or
  another Demon steps in; before the first babysitter, having no Demon is no win for good.
- Ruling 147: the token stays with its player through any change until the next choice, except a Snake Charmer swap,
  where it goes with the Minion character.
- Ruling 148: the seven jinxes, for example with a living Magician the Storyteller picks the babysitter, and a
  babysitting Psychopath or Vizier simply dies when executed.
- Rulings 149 and 150: the core changes do nothing on scripts without Lil' Monsta, and its test script deals at every
  size from 5 to 15 with all its jinxes.

### The Atheist (items 85 to 88)

**C6.5. Keep the Atheist at all? (rulings 151 to 156; switch: atheist)** The Atheist is a Townsfolk whose ability says
the Storyteller can break the game rules, and if executed the good team wins; there is no evil team at all. Engine now:
built, with a bounded set of rule breaks, because the honest version, a Storyteller free to break any rule, can't be
run by an engine. It makes two more corpus scripts play. Question: keep it, or remove it? Options:
one, keep it; two, remove it, since the engine's version is a reading rather than the character's real spirit. Note:
Riot's Atheist jinx is declared only while the Atheist is built, so removing the Atheist leaves Riot working.

**C6.6. The rules the Atheist's Storyteller breaks (ruling 153).** Engine now: exactly three breaks. First, before the
first night the Storyteller picks a fake evil team, a Demon from the script and the usual number of Minions, written
on the Grimoire for itself only. Second, every piece of information may be anything; the scripted Storyteller shows
what the fake Grimoire would show nine times in ten, otherwise the truth, and every player's false picture is that same
fake team, so the lies agree with each other. Third, each night but the first, a player may die as if a Demon killed
them, which the Monk and Soldier still stop. Nothing else is broken: abilities work, the fake team is really good, and
executing the fake Demon does nothing special. Question: is that the right set? Options: one, as now; two, more breaks,
such as telling a player they are evil, faking a Demon's death and a win, or making nominations fail; three, fewer,
truth only, which would give the Atheist away at once.

**C6.7. Executing the Storyteller, and when no Atheist is in play (ruling 154).** On any script with the Atheist on
it, in play or not, anyone may nominate the Storyteller once a day, and the vote runs as for a player. If the
Storyteller is executed, the game ends: good wins if the Atheist is in play, alive or dead. Engine now: if the Atheist
is not in play, executing the Storyteller is evil's win, a reading of the almanac's How to Run. Question: is evil's win
right? Options: one, evil wins, as now, so bluffing the Atheist and getting the Storyteller executed is a real way for
evil to win; two, nothing happens when the Atheist isn't in play, and the day goes on.

**C6.8. Confirm these in one go: the Atheist's deal and its end (rulings 151, 152, 155 and 156).**
- Ruling 151: when the Atheist is drawn, every evil seat goes to a Townsfolk not in play, an Outsider if none is left,
  and the Outsiders are the table's; a draw that leaves fewer than three bluffs is drawn again. A Drunk who believes
  they are the Atheist changes nothing.
- Ruling 152: with the Atheist in play there is no Demon, and that is no win for good; the game goes on until the
  Storyteller is executed or two players remain, which is evil's win, "good never executed the Storyteller", though
  nobody is evil.
- Ruling 155: the core changes, the Storyteller's lies and a nominee who is not a player, do nothing on other scripts.
- Ruling 156: its test script deals at every size from 5 to 15, with and without the Atheist.

### The Wizard (items 89 to 91)

**C6.9. Keep the Wizard at all? (rulings 157 to 161; switch: wizard)** The Wizard is a Minion who makes one wish,
which the Storyteller may grant, perhaps at a price, and perhaps leaving a clue. Engine now: built with a curated list
of wishes the engine can actually grant, each with its own prices; it has no official jinx, and it makes two more
corpus scripts play. Question: keep it, or remove it? Options: one, keep it with a fixed list; two,
remove it, since a Wizard limited to a list is a much smaller character than the real one.

**C6.10. The Wizard's wish list and its prices (ruling 157).** Engine now: eight wishes, each with a few possible
prices. The prices are: the Wizard is poisoned for two days and nights; exposed, meaning a good player learns the
Wizard is a Minion; the Wizard dies tonight; a good player learns the Demon is one of two players; the Demon is
poisoned tonight and tomorrow; or told, meaning the player the wish named, or a good player, learns the Wizard is the
Wizard. The wishes: a chosen living player dies tonight, priced poisoned, exposed or the Wizard dies; the Demon can't
die until the Wizard's next night, priced poisoned, exposed or the Demon's two players; a chosen living player turns
evil and is told, priced the Wizard dies, the Demon poisoned, or exposed; the Wizard learns a chosen player's
character, priced told or exposed; the Wizard sees the Grimoire tonight, priced exposed or poisoned; a chosen living
player is drunk for three days and nights, priced poisoned or exposed; a dead evil player comes back to life, priced
the Wizard dies or the Demon's two players; and the Wizard learns the Demon's three bluffs, priced exposed or told.
Left out: wishes the engine can't run, such as a new character, a changed rule or extra votes, and wishes that would
end the game at once. Question: is the list right, and are the prices fair? Options: a longer list; prices of the
owner's choosing; drop a wish that seems too strong, such as bringing back a dead evil player.

**C6.11. How a wish is made and granted (rulings 158 to 161).** Engine now: each night, the first included, a living
Wizard who hasn't wished is shown the numbered list and may pick one, naming a player if needed, or wait. A wish in
the player's own words is matched to the list by the Storyteller, who can only pick a listed wish or "none", never
invent one. "None", or a wish with no fitting player, means no wish, so the Wizard can try another night. Once made,
the wish is spent. Then three Storyteller calls, each among listed values only: whether it's granted, nearly always for
the scripted Storyteller; its price, usually one; and its clue, either announced to the town at dawn, whispered as the
wish's words to a good player, or none. The Wizard is told whether it was granted and the price. A drunk or poisoned
Wizard's wish isn't granted and is still spent. Question: is that right? Options: let a drunk Wizard keep the wish for
later; let the clue name the price too; let a model judge propose a wish off the list.

### Legion (items 92 to 95)

**C6.12. Keep Legion at all? (rulings 162 to 169; switch: legion)** Legion is a Demon that most of the players are,
with no Minions; executions fail if only evil players voted, and it registers as a Minion too. Engine now: built, with
all eight official jinxes, including the Engineer's that batch 4 left waiting; it makes one more corpus script play.
Question: keep it, or remove it? Note: about a quarter of random-bot games on its test script reach the
tenth day unfinished, because random good votes rarely carry an execution past Legion's rule; with real players that
may be different.

**C6.13. How many players are Legion, and small tables (ruling 162 and U15).** Legion's text says "most players are
Legion". Engine now: a bare majority, half the players rounded down plus one, so three at five or six players and
eight at fifteen. The fuzzer found that at five or six players, three or four Legion leave the Noble no group of three
with exactly one evil player, and the Knight no two players who aren't the Demon, so they have nothing true to learn.
That's a rules impossibility of the deal, like the earlier Noble findings in group 2. Question: what count, and what at
small tables? Options for the count: one, a bare majority, as now; two, the almanac's table;
three, the Storyteller's choice within limits. Options for small tables: deal fewer Legion, for example two at five; or
the Noble and Knight learn nothing, as for the earlier Noble findings.

**C6.14. Legion registers as a Minion only to the Investigator (ruling 166).** Legion's text says "you register as a
Minion too". Engine now: only the Investigator sees a Legion as a Minion, shown as a Minion of the script, and that is
true information. Everywhere else Legion registers only as the Demon, so the Slayer, the Knight and the Fortune Teller
see the Demon and nothing else. Why: the engine's registration can't hold "a Demon and a Minion at once"; it would
read as "either one, the Storyteller's call", which would let the Slayer miss, and the text doesn't allow that. With no
Minions dealt, the Investigator otherwise had nothing to show and the game stopped. Question: is that right? Options:
one, as now; two, register as a Minion to every ability that asks about Minions.

**C6.15. Confirm these in one go: Legion's other rules (rulings 163, 164, 165, 167, 168 and 169).**
- Ruling 163: each Legion learns the others and the bluffs; the Legion don't wake to choose.
- Ruling 164: each night but the first, one kill for all of them, any living player who isn't Legion, or nobody; with
  only Legion left, one of them dies, so the game comes down to two. With every living Legion drunk, poisoned or
  exorcised, nobody dies.
- Ruling 165: an execution fails when the vote that put the player on the block had at least one vote, every voter
  registered as evil to Legion, and a living Legion is sober; a Spy or Recluse is the Storyteller's call. An
  execution without a vote, such as madness or the Vizier's, isn't "only evil voted".
- Ruling 167: the eight jinxes, for example no tea party at all while Legion is in play, whoever has the Hatter's
  ability; and when Legion is created any way, the Pit-Hag's included as a reading, every evil player becomes Legion.
- Rulings 168 and 169: the core changes do nothing on other scripts, and its test script deals at every size with all
  its jinxes.

### Riot (items 96 to 99)

**C6.16. Keep Riot at all? (rulings 170 to 175; switch: riot)** Riot is a Demon that kills nobody at night; on day 3
the Minions become Riot too, and every nominee dies at once but must nominate again. Engine now: built, with all twelve
official jinxes, the Atheist's declared only while the Atheist is built, so either can be removed; it makes one more
corpus script play. Question: keep it, or remove it? Note: every game on its test script ends by day 3.

**C6.17. Which Riot text: the older one, or "after day 3, evil wins" (rulings 170 to 172, and the jinx reading in
173).** Engine now: the text in the official data, "On day 3, Minions become Riot and nominees die but nominate an
alive player immediately. This must happen." Days 1 and 2 play as usual, and executing Riot then wins for good. On the
third night the living Minions become Riot. Day 3's nominations are the riot: each nominee is executed at once without
a vote, then must nominate an alive player not yet nominated today, until the game ends or nobody is left to name; then
the day goes on, and later days are as usual. The newer text adds "after day 3, evil wins". Also, four of Riot's jinxes,
with the Banshee, Farmer, Ravenkeeper and Sage, speak of a nightly choice this text doesn't have; engine now: with one
of those on the script, the first living Riot chooses an alive player each night but the first, a reading. Question:
which text? Options: one, the older text, as now; two, the newer text, so evil wins at the end of day 3 if good hasn't
won. What follows: in practice the riot usually runs until the game ends anyway, so the choice matters mainly when it
stops early.

**C6.18. Riot: the Storyteller names a nominee for a player. This departs from the owner's rule; take it slowly
(ruling 172).** The owner ruled for the other scripts that a required choice is never made for the player: it is asked
again with the reason, and after four reminders a seat that still gives no legal answer stops the game as a failed seat
does. Engine now, for Riot only: a nominee who must nominate, or the Riot who must start the riot when nobody does, is
asked once; if they give no legal player, the Storyteller names one for them at once, a judged call among the alive
players they may nominate. Why: the text says "this must happen", and a riot with a stalled player would hang the day.
Question: may the engine choose for the player here? Options: one, as now, the Storyteller names one after a single
ask; two, the usual rule, asked again with the reason up to four times, then a failed seat; three, the riot ends when
a player gives no legal nominee, as when nobody is left to name.

**C6.19. Confirm these in one go: Riot's other rules (rulings 171, 173, 174 and 175).**
- Ruling 171: on the third night every living Minion becomes Riot and is told; the dead stay as they are. Good wins
  when every Riot is dead.
- Ruling 173, protections: if a Riot nominates, and so executes, the player a working Exorcist, Innkeeper or Monk chose
  last night, or a working Soldier, good wins at once.
- Ruling 173, Grandmother: with a living Riot, the Grandchild dying by execution is evil's win.
- Ruling 173, King: with Riot in play and a player dead, a King not told by their own rule tonight learns an alive
  character at Riot's step.
- Ruling 173, Mayor: before each riot nomination, a living working Mayor is asked whether to stop the riot; yes ends
  the game, good's win with one Riot alive, else evil's.
- Ruling 173, Atheist: the Storyteller may be nominated in the riot; players vote as usual, and if it isn't executed
  the nominator nominates again.
- Rulings 174 and 175: the core changes do nothing on other scripts, and its test script, with Riot as the only Demon,
  deals at every size.
